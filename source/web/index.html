AWS SOW Validator

Catch a broken Scope of Work before your customer does. Problem

AWS Partners and pre-sales teams sign off Scopes of Work under deadline pressure, usually without a second technical reviewer. That first pass only catches what a tired reader remembers to check — not whether every row in the cost table actually sums to the stated total, whether the architecture accidentally carries a client name from a different account, or whether a service pairing has ever been validated against a known-good pattern. Once the SOW is approved, there's rarely a reproducible record of why.

  • Cost tables are hand-totalled, and arithmetic mistakes travel straight into signed commercial documents.
  • Architecture and service integrations are eyeballed, not checked against known-good and known-bad wiring patterns.
  • There's no artifact recording what was reviewed and why it passed, so approvals aren't reproducible or auditable.

Checks

Segment & industry rule packs

Deterministic checks tuned by segment (Enterprise, SMB, Digital Native) and industry (FSI, Retail, generic), so the bar is right for the deal in front of you.

Cost-table arithmetic

Every row is re-summed and compared to the stated monthly and annual totals, to the cent — no rounding, no benefit of the doubt.

Brand-contamination scan

Catches another client's name left over in paragraphs, tables, headers, footers, or captions from a copy-pasted template.

Deprecated-service detection

Flags AWS services named in the SOW that are deprecated or on a retirement path, before a customer signs up to build on them.

Well-Architected gap analysis

Checks the design against all six Well-Architected pillars — Operational Excellence, Security, Reliability, Performance Efficiency, Cost Optimization, and Sustainability.

Integration chaining

Classifies every service-to-service pairing as native, glue-required, anti-pattern, or unverified, against a fixed service graph.

SOW scoring rubric

Scores objectives, scope, acceptance criteria, assumptions, timeline, commercials, roles, and architecture alignment against a weighted rubric.

How this is built

AWS SOW Validator runs a two-tier review. Tier 1 is fully deterministic — it runs the moment a document is dropped, with no model call, no network round trip beyond loading this page, and no cost. Tier 2 adds an agent-assisted review, powered by Amazon Nova on Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Runtime, for teams that want a deeper, model-informed pass.

In Tier 2 the model's job is narrow by design: it reads the SOW and proposes what it sees. Every number in the report — cost lines, arithmetic deltas, severity counts — is computed by Python, not the model. Every integration verdict (native, glue-required, anti-pattern, unverified) is decided by a rule pack against a fixed service graph. Every citation is a real AWS documentation URL resolved by code, never invented text. The model proposes; the pipeline decides.
